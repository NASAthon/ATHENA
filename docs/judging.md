### A. Project Submission Start & End Times
* **Opens:** Saturday, November 14, 2026, at **9:00 a.m.** (Local Event time zone).
* **Closes:** Sunday, November 15, 2026, at **11:59 p.m.** (Local Event time zone).
* **Universal Event Exception:** Follows the local time zone of your **Team Owner's** area of residence.
* *Note:* Always confirm with your Local Lead (e.g., HackAthlone) for local pitch/judging deadlines, which may occur earlier on Sunday than the global portal lock time.

### B. Editing Your Project Submission Page & File Hosting Rules
* **Where to Edit:** Profile Icon (top right) $\rightarrow$ **My Team** $\rightarrow$ **Project** tab (appears only after 9:00 a.m. local time on Nov 14).
* **Saving & Locking:**
  * Click **Edit** to begin and **Save Progress** frequently. Changes remain private to your team until you officially click **Submit for Judging**.
  * Toggle the **Lock** switch to `ON` while editing so teammates know not to overwrite your unsaved changes (though teammates can manually override the lock).
* **Direct File Upload Limits:**
  * **Allowed directly on page:** Images only (`JPG`, `PNG`, `GIF` up to **10MB**).
  * **NOT allowed directly on page:** Videos or documents (PDFs, PPTX, etc.).
* **External Hosting Rules:**
  * Upload demos, slide decks, videos, and code to public external hosts (YouTube, Google Drive, GitHub, OneDrive, Dropbox).
  * **Zero Login/Permission Barriers:** Set all links to public access and test them in an Incognito/Private browser window. If judges cannot open a link immediately, the project cannot be evaluated.
  * **Custom Websites (FortiGuard Web Filter):** If hosting your project on a newly registered custom domain, NASA recommends submitting a classification request via the [FortiGuard Web Filter Classification form](https://www.fortiguard.com/webfilter) (set Company Name to `"N/A"` and choose a relevant category like *Education* or *Web-based Application*) so corporate/government firewalls do not block judges from viewing your site.

### C. Project Page Form Field Instructions
1. **Project Name:** Short, memorable title for your project.
2. **High-Level Project Summary:** What you built, how it solves the challenge, and why it matters.
3. **Link to Project Demo (CRITICAL):**
   * Must be **either** a **30-second (or less) video** OR a **slide deck of 7 slides maximum** (including title and introductory slides).
   * Must be publicly accessible without login/password.
   * Directly impacts your **Presentation** score; exceeding the 30-second or 7-slide cap **disqualifies** the project from Global Judging.
4. **Link to Final Project:** Public link to your live app, interactive prototype, repository (GitHub), or cloud folder showcasing the working solution.
5. **Detailed Project Description:**
   * What does it do, and how does it work?
   * What benefits does it provide, and what do you hope to achieve?
   * What tools, coding languages, hardware, or software were used?
6. **Use of Super Intelligence (SI / AI Tools):**
   * *Policy:* AI/LLMs, chatbots, ML, and image generators are **permitted** to accelerate development and execution, **but should NOT be used to generate your core idea** of how to solve the challenge.
   * *Branding Restriction:* AI-generated content **must not** use, contain, or modify official NASA logos, flags, or mission identifiers.
   * *Mandatory Attribution:*
     * **Images & Videos:** Must display a **visible watermark** indicating AI/SI generation.
     * **Audio:** Must acknowledge AI/SI generation in descriptive text and metadata.
     * **Code & Data:** Must acknowledge AI/SI generation in descriptive text and metadata.
   * *Note:* The form's dedicated SI checkbox/text field is for NASA internal data collection and is hidden from judges, **but judges WILL see and evaluate** your visible watermarks, credits, and descriptive disclosures inside your project.
7. **NASA Data:**
   * Specify the exact NASA datasets and resources used. **You must use at least one official NASA dataset/resource** to be eligible for Global Awards.
8. **Space Agency Partner Data and Other References:**
   * Cite all external code libraries, open-source tools, text, images, and any 2026 Space Agency Partner datasets used (e.g., ESA, CSA, JAXA, ISRO, UK Space Agency, etc.). Ensure you have permission for any copyrighted material.

### D. Tips for Creating a Project Demo
* **Language Requirement:** All submission text, slide decks, and linked materials **must be in English**. (Exception: Video demos may feature spoken audio in another language *only if* English subtitles are included).
* **Recommended 30-Second Video Pacing:**
  * `[0:00]` Introduce yourself and your team name.
  * `[0:02]` State the challenge you chose and what inspired you.
  * `[0:05]` Announce your project title and tagline.
  * `[0:10]` Explain how it works, show the prototype/UI, and highlight how NASA/Partner open data powers it.
  * `[0:20–0:30]` Paint the future vision and real-world/space impact.
* **Recommended 7-Slide Deck Checklist:**
  * Include: **Team Name**, **Team Member Names**, and **Selected Challenge**.
  * Include visuals of your working project/UI, user experience flow, and how NASA open data makes the solution possible.
  * Keep text concise and fonts large/readable; strictly **$\le 7$ slides total**.

### E. Submitting Your Project & Participant Certificates
* **Final Submission Checklist:**
  1. Every team member is confirmed at the **same Local Event** (not waitlisted) or all under the Universal Event.
  2. All required fields are filled out in English.
  3. No images, video, audio, or likeness of anyone **under 18 years old** appear in demos or project files.
  4. Check both confirmation boxes (**Terms & Conditions** and **Confirm All Work is Original**).
  5. Click **Submit for Judging** before **Sunday, Nov 15 at 11:59 p.m.** (you can edit and re-submit multiple times until the deadline; only the final saved submission is judged).
* **Participant Certificates:** Automatically generated as a PDF (format in **Letter** size `8.5x11in` on desktop) under **Profile $\rightarrow$ Participant Information** only for teams that click **Submit for Judging** on time.

### A. Eligibility Requirements for Global Judging
To advance from a Local Event (like HackAthlone) to Global Judging, your team must satisfy all of the following:
1. **Official Challenge:** Directly address one of the official 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenges.
2. **Team Roster & Event Alignment:**
   * Local Event teams: **1 to 6 members** (Universal Event teams: **2 to 6 members**).
   * **All teammates (including Team Owner) must be registered and confirmed at the exact same Local Event** (no waitlisted members; no adding members after submission closes).
3. **Complete Submission & Demo Limits:** All required fields complete in English, with a demo strictly within the **7-slide** or **30-second video** limit, submitted before **Nov 15 at 11:59 p.m. local time**.
4. **Originality & Compliance Agreements:** Both legal/originality checkboxes checked.
5. **NASA China Funding Restriction Policy:** Teams undergo eligibility screening prohibiting bilateral activity or affiliation with the government of China, Hong Kong, or Macau, or any entity/company/university owned or incorporated under their laws.

---

### B. Official Judging Criteria (1–5 Scale per Category)
Every round of judging (Local, Global Nominee, and Executive) scores projects from **1 to 5** across five equally weighted pillars:

| Criterion | What Judges Look For (Score of 5 Benchmark) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Impact** | Addresses a major real-world or spaceflight problem with extensive reach, practical benefit, and high potential to inspire or help a broad audience. |
| **2. Creativity** | Originality, novelty, and inventive execution; introduces a fresh perspective or meaningful breakthrough over conventional approaches. |
| **3. Validity** | Grounded in sound scientific principles; demonstrates technical, functional, and logical feasibility, completeness, and strong user-friendliness/usability. |
| **4. Relevance** | Directly answers the chosen challenge's goals and **meaningfully integrates NASA open data as a core functional engine** (not a superficial add-on). |
| **5. Presentation** | Clear, well-structured storytelling across your demo and project page that persuasively communicates the problem, solution, and significance. |

---

### C. Judging Timeline & 10 Global Award Categories
* **Phase 1 — Local Event Judging & Global Nominees (Announced Dec 3, 2026):** Local panels evaluate all local submissions using the 5 criteria above and nominate top teams to advance to Global Judging (separate from any local venue prizes).
* **Phase 2 — Global Finalists & Honorable Mentions (Announced Dec 17, 2026):** Subject-matter expert judges narrow Global Nominees down to **Global Finalists** (and recognize non-advancing **Honorable Mentions**).
* **Phase 3 — Global Winners (Announced Jan 26, 2027):** Executive judges from NASA and Space Agency Partners select one winner for each of the **10 Global Awards**:
  1. **Best Use of Science:** Best and most valid application of the scientific method.
  2. **Best Use of Data:** Best makes space data accessible or applies it uniquely.
  3. **Best Use of Technology:** Most innovative use of technology.
  4. **Galactic Impact:** Greatest potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe.
  5. **Best Mission Concept:** Most plausible mission concept and design.
  6. **Most Inspirational:** Project that best captures hearts and inspires.
  7. **Best Storytelling Award:** Most creatively communicates open data through storytelling.
  8. **Global Connection Award:** Best connects people worldwide through technology.
  9. **Art & Technology Award:** Most effectively combines technical and creative skills.
  10. **Local Impact Award:** Greatest potential to make a difference in your local community.
